Cellphones are common in South African schools, but learners and teachers do not always agree about using them in class. I think they should be allowed for planned activities only. A complete ban may remove a useful tool, while unrestricted use can distract the class. Teachers should decide when a phone supports the lesson.

For example, learners can use a dictionary app to check the meaning of a word. They could also complete a short quiz chosen by the teacher. These activities can save time, but the phone should not replace careful reading or discussion. Learners should explain what they learned instead of simply copying information from a screen.

There must be another option for learners who do not have a device or data. A printed worksheet or shared school tablet can make the activity more equal. If only learners with personal phones can take part, the policy is not fair. Schools should also explain the rules to families so that everyone understands them.

Phones can interrupt learning when messages arrive. Cameras can also cause privacy problems if a learner takes a photo without permission. Teachers can ask learners to keep phones silent and put them away when they are not needed. A consistent rule across classes would help learners know what to expect.

Some people believe phones should never be allowed because they cause distraction. This is a serious concern, but teaching responsible use can also prepare learners for work and further study. The school should set limits and apply them fairly.

In conclusion, phones should be allowed only for a clear learning purpose and with teacher permission. This approach supports useful activities while protecting attention and privacy. Schools should review the rule with learners and families when it is not working.
