Schools should allow cellphones in classrooms only when a teacher has chosen a purposeful activity. A total ban ignores the ways a phone can support learning, but unrestricted access treats every moment as suitable for screen use. A clear, teacher-led policy can protect attention while helping learners practise responsible technology use.

Consider a Grade 10 learner who meets an unfamiliar word while reading an English passage. A dictionary app can give a quick definition, after which the learner can explain the word in context. A teacher-selected quiz can also show which ideas need another explanation. In both cases the phone serves a specific learning goal; it does not replace the teacher's feedback or the learner's own reasoning.

However, a phone policy must not make access to learning depend on family income. Some learners have no device, limited data or an unreliable connection. A school should offer printed materials or a shared device before an activity begins. That alternative is not an optional kindness: it is necessary for a fair classroom where every learner can complete the same task.

Distraction and privacy are genuine risks. A notification can interrupt a difficult explanation, and taking a photograph without permission can harm another learner. Phones should remain silent and out of sight unless the teacher has invited their use. The school should explain consequences in advance and apply them consistently, rather than expecting each teacher to invent a different rule.

Supporters of a complete ban may argue that it is easier to enforce. Simplicity matters, especially when a class is large, but a carefully limited rule is still clear: teacher permission is required, and phones are away at other times. Learners gain practice in making responsible choices without being given unlimited access.

Therefore, cellphones should be permitted for planned learning activities, with non-digital alternatives and firm privacy rules. This balanced policy keeps the lesson in control of the teacher, includes learners with different resources and teaches a practical skill for life beyond school.
